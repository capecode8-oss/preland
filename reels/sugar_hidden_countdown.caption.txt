Quick test. Think about everything you ate before noon today. Now count the spoons of sugar. Be honest.

Here's why that's hard. Sugar hides in food that doesn't taste sweet. Ketchup. Pasta sauce. A granola bar. A fruit yogurt with the word "healthy" printed right on the cup. The numbers change by brand, so check your own label. But the math never changes: every 4 grams of sugar is about 1 teaspoon.

The American Heart Association says women should stop at 6 teaspoons (25 grams) of added sugar a day. National data from 2017 to 2018 put the average American woman at about 15. Nobody pours 15 spoons into their coffee. It's in the sauce, the bar, the yogurt.

Here's the part most people miss. In one small study, people ate way less sugar for three months. Their food tasted sweeter. But they still wanted the same amount of sweet. So cutting sugar isn't only about willpower. It starts with knowing your number.

How to find it:
1. Turn the package around.
2. Find the line that says Added Sugars.
3. Divide the grams by 4. That's your spoons.

Do it for one day. That's the whole trick.
