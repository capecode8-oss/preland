The headline said winning the lottery ends women's marriages. The study says something more careful.

Economists Cesarini, Lindqvist, Östling and Terskaya studied Swedish lottery players, which is a rare way to test what sudden money does to a marriage, because winning is random. Their paper appeared in the Journal of Human Resources.

What they found:

1. Men who won married more
Male winners were more likely to get married and to have children, and their divorce risk likely went down.

2. Women who won divorced sooner
The only clear effect on female winners was a higher risk of divorce in the short run. It did not last in the long run.

3. The money sped up an ending
The short-run jump was biggest in recently married couples, where the match was predicted to be weak and the husband was the main earner. The authors read it as money speeding up marriages that were already ending, not creating the problem.

The honest limits: this is Swedish data, the divorce effect was short-term, and it says nothing about why each couple split. It is one study, not a rule for every marriage.

(Source: Cesarini, Lindqvist, Östling and Terskaya, "Fortunate Families?", Journal of Human Resources, as reported by PsyPost.)
