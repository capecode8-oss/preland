Here's the part the "miracle water" posts leave out.

The test used water on purpose contaminated with bacteria. Not clean tap water.

Researchers put 500 bacteria per milliliter into copper pots. After 16 hours at room temperature, they found no living bacteria they could grow. That included E. coli, Salmonella, Shigella and cholera.

Here's what that does and doesn't mean:

1. It was a lab test
Not a trial on people. The authors suggested it might help places with unsafe water. They did not say it's a health hack.

2. The copper stayed low
About 177 parts per billion, well within the WHO limit. Nothing wild happened to the water.

3. Some bacteria may be hurt, not killed
A later study found some Salmonella and cholera cells were damaged rather than fully dead. Science is still sorting that out.

4. Acid is where copper turns on you
Lemon, lime, ginger beer, vinegar. The FDA Food Code, which many US states follow, says acidic drinks shouldn't touch unlined copper. Iowa banned copper mugs for Moscow Mules in 2017.

In a small Montana study, a Moscow Mule pulled more copper than the EPA's drinking water limit within 27 minutes. One drink probably won't hurt you. Daily habits are another story.

If you love copper: plain water only, and look for stainless steel lining if you add anything sour.

Honest note: most US tap water is already treated. You don't need copper to make it safe.
