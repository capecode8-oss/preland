Most of those six years ended the same way: clouds, or no alignment at all.

Valerio Minato first had the idea in 2017. He said only about two days a year gave him a real shot. Some years the Moon, the mountain and the basilica never lined up. Weather ruined the rest. One write-up says he tried five times over six years.

On December 15, 2023, just outside Turin, Italy, it finally worked. The Moon sat right behind Monviso. The Basilica of Superga glowed in front.

That's the kind of patience most of us only dream of.

Here's what makes this photo special:

1. The odds were awful
Two real chances a year. Add bad weather. Add six years of trying.

2. NASA noticed
It became NASA's Astronomy Picture of the Day on Christmas 2023.

3. The dark part of the Moon is lit
The exposure was long enough to show "da Vinci glow." That's sunlight bouncing off Earth, hitting the Moon, and bouncing back to us.

4. He didn't change the plan
He didn't switch to an easier shot. He waited for the right night and kept showing up.
