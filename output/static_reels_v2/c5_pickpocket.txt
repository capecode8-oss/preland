Most pickpocketing in busy tourist areas starts with a distraction. A spilled drink. A friendly stranger. A long chat. Canada's government travel advice warns about exactly that for Italy.

One city guide describes teams of 2 to 6 people with set roles: one distracts, one lifts, one walks away with it. That's a commercial guide, not official data. But a Milan airport case reported by The Local involved a gang of 3.

Here's what to do the moment you notice:

1. Stop and check what's gone
Wallet, phone, passport. Say it out loud. It helps you think straight.

2. Block your cards first
Do it before anything else. Every minute counts.

3. Call 112
That's the emergency number across Italy. Or tell any police officer nearby, or go to a Carabinieri or Polizia di Stato station.

4. Get a police report
You'll almost always need it for travel insurance. For minor theft, a preliminary complaint can be filed online, in Italian.

5. Don't chase
Honest note, my opinion: no wallet is worth a fight. I couldn't find an official statement that tells tourists to confront thieves, and a wrist grab can turn ugly fast.

Prevention is boring but works. Zip bags and keep them in front of you. Keep valuables in inside pockets. Be extra careful on buses, trains, stations and around famous sights.

None of this is fancy. All of it works better than luck.
