Most of what a man releases is made in two glands most people never think about.

And neither of them makes sperm.

Sound strange? It's in every physiology textbook.

Sperm are only about 5 to 10 percent of the volume. The rest is a fluid built for one job: keeping them alive long enough to matter.

Here's what's actually in it:

1. A sugar called fructose
Made in the seminal vesicles, which supply roughly two thirds of the fluid. Fructose is the fuel sperm burn to swim.

2. Zinc, and a lot of it
Levels are over 100 times higher than in blood plasma. The prostate adds it, along with citric acid and enzymes. It makes about 25 to 30 percent of the fluid.

3. A built-in gel switch
It comes out thick like a gel. Within about 15 to 30 minutes an enzyme from the prostate turns it watery. That's by design, and completely normal.

4. A chemical buffer
It's slightly alkaline. That helps sperm survive an acidic environment they would otherwise not last in.

Also in there: amino acids, prostaglandins, and dozens of other proteins. Scientists are still working out what some of them do.

Honest note: this is basic physiology from standard textbooks and medical reviews. Ranges vary from man to man and day to day. None of it is medical advice.
