Four or five raw beans can be enough to make an adult sick.

And your slow cooker might not fix that.

Red kidney beans carry a natural toxin called phytohaemagglutinin. It's in the highest amounts in raw red kidney beans. Food safety agencies in the US and UK both warn about it.

Symptoms usually hit within 1 to 3 hours: nausea, vomiting, diarrhea. Most people recover in a few hours. It's rarely dangerous. But it is miserable.

Here's how to do it right:

1. Soak them
At least 5 hours. Then pour that water out. Don't cook in it.

2. Boil hard in fresh water
A full rolling boil for at least 10 minutes. Many cooks do 30 to be safe.

3. Skip the slow cooker shortcut
The FDA's food safety guide warns that beans held at around 175°F can end up more toxic than raw ones. A slow cooker may never reach a real boil.

4. Or buy canned
Canned beans are already cooked. You can eat them straight away.

Honest note: this is about red kidney beans. Most other beans carry far less of it. Not a reason to fear beans. Just cook them properly, every single time.

And they're worth it. A half cup of cooked kidney beans has roughly 6 to 7 grams of fiber.

Most women in the US get around 15 grams a day. The usual recommendation is 25 under 50, and 21 over 50. Fewer than 1 in 10 adults reach it, by most estimates.
