Two cabins on the same ship can feel like two different cruises.

Ships move in two ways. Pitch is the front-to-back rocking. Roll is the side-to-side sway. Travel guides say cabins at the very front or very back feel pitch the most, and higher decks feel more roll. The usual advice for people who get seasick is a cabin near the middle of the ship on a lower deck. The exact deck changes from ship to ship, so check the deck plan.

What the sources I checked suggest:

1. Go midship
The middle of the ship moves the least from front to back.

2. Go lower, but not the lowest
Lower decks soften the roll. Some guides warn that the very bottom decks can bring more engine noise and vibration.

3. Skip the inside cabin if you get seasick
Guides and forum travelers say a window or balcony helps. If you can see the horizon, keep your eyes on it. That one is common advice, not a tested result.

4. Know what has evidence
Scopolamine and some antihistamines have been found in studies to work better than placebo. Side effects include drowsiness and dry mouth, so ask your doctor, especially if you take other medicines. Ginger and wristbands have mixed results.

5. Feeling off? Stay in the middle
Sources suggest staying near the middle of the ship and not going below deck to lie down once you feel sick.

Honest note: sources disagree on how low to go, and a 2012 forum poster pointed out that on sea days you'll be all over the ship, so location alone won't fix everything.
