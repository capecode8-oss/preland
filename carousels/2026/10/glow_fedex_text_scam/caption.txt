You may really be expecting a package. That's why it works.

Police and attorneys general keep warning about the "FedEx" text scam. A text says a package couldn't be delivered and gives you a tracking code and a link to reschedule.

What the warnings say:

1. The link is the trap
In the FTC's account, it leads to a page posing as a customer survey that offers a prize, then asks for a credit card number to cover "shipping and handling." Some versions sign you up for expensive services.

2. FedEx says it didn't send it
FedEx says it doesn't send unsolicited texts or emails asking for money, packages or personal information.

3. Other carriers get used too
Scammers also pretend to be UPS, USPS and Amazon, so a text naming another carrier can be the same scheme.

(Sources: FTC consumer alert; Idaho and Florida attorneys general; FedEx guidance. Most of these warnings date from 2020 to 2022, so check FedEx's current page for the latest advice.)

What to do: delete the text without opening the link. If you're expecting something, type the tracking number directly into FedEx's website. If it isn't recognized, it isn't real. You can forward the message to abuse@fedex.com.
