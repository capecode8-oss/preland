Night leg cramps are one of those things almost everyone over 50 has had, and everyone has an opinion about. The usual advice is magnesium and stretching. So what does the evidence say?

What the reviews found:

1. Magnesium
A Cochrane review of mostly older adults found magnesium's effect on cramp frequency compared with placebo was small and not statistically significant. The certainty was moderate.

2. Stretching
A Cochrane review of three studies (201 people aged 50 and older) found low or very low certainty evidence that stretching reduces leg cramps.

3. Mixed results
One 2012 trial of 80 adults over 55 found bedtime stretching reduced cramps over six weeks. Earlier reviews found no difference. The evidence disagrees with itself.

The honest limits: the stretching review's searches ran up to 2020, and I couldn't check whether it has been updated. None of this means magnesium never helps anyone. It means the trials didn't show a reliable effect for cramps.

(Sources: Cochrane reviews on magnesium and on stretching for muscle cramps; AAFP summary, 2017.)

If cramps are frequent, severe, or come with swelling or weakness, tell your doctor. Cramps can have causes worth looking into. Keep a quick note of how often they happen and what you did that day.
