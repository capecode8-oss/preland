Third night of the cruise, the ship's nurse told me she sees the same thing every sailing.

People use the sanitizer at the buffet door, feel safe, and eat.

Here's what she said. Norovirus is the one that ruins a cruise, and alcohol sanitizer doesn't handle it well. Soap, water, and about twenty seconds does. The dispensers at the door look reassuring and they help with other germs. But for the one that sinks vacations, it's the sink that matters.

That was myth number one. She gave me four more.

The ship's medical center isn't included in your fare. Visits are billed to your cabin account, often at hundreds of dollars, and a lot of regular health plans don't cover care at sea.

If you miss the ship in port, it leaves anyway. All-aboard times are real. You pay to catch up at the next stop. The tours booked through the ship are the ones that wait for you.

Ice isn't safe just because it's frozen. Freezing doesn't kill germs. On shore, if you can't tell where the water came from, skip the ice.

And the handrails. Thousands of hands touch them every day. A quick wipe moves germs around more than it removes them. Wash your hands before you eat, not after.

None of this is dramatic. That's the point. A cruise feels like the most controlled kind of travel there is, so nobody questions the small stuff.
