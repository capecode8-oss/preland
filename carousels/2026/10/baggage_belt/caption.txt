On a crowded belt, every black bag is a twin. Here's the quick check that tells yours from the copy.

Most of us watch the belt for the shape of our bag. That's the mistake. An etiquette expert quoted by The Points Guy put it simply: "Check the tags before walking away. All black bags look the same."

Here's what the travel sources I checked agree on:

1. Read the name on the airline's tag
The long white tag the airline put on your bag shows your name. Your own tag can fall off. The airline's tag is the fallback.

2. Keep the tag
Don't rip old tags off at the carousel. You may need them later to support a claim if something is damaged or missing.

3. Photograph it before you check in
A photo of the bag and its tags helps you describe it later. Keep the tag stub too, because some airports ask for it before they hand a bag back. Put your contact details inside the bag as well.

4. If your bag never shows, report it before you leave
Go to the airline's baggage desk and file a report before you walk out of the airport. A DOT rule says that on domestic flights, if a checked bag isn't delivered within 12 hours of arrival and you filed a report, the airline must refund the bag fee. International windows are 15 or 30 hours depending on flight length.

Honest note: sources I found disagree on exact report deadlines, and I couldn't confirm the date the refund rule took effect. Check the DOT's lost-baggage page and your airline's contract of carriage for your trip.
