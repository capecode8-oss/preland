She didn't worry about turbulence. She worried about the people sleeping.

I was on an overnight flight to Lisbon, wide awake at 2 AM, when a flight attendant stopped by my row with a glass of water. Thirty years in the air. I asked what bothered her most about long flights. She looked down the dark aisle and said, "The ones who don't move."

Here's what she told me.

1. Sitting still slows your legs
Sit for hours and the blood in your legs moves slower. On long trips, that can lead to a blood clot. Health sources point to trips over four hours as the point where it counts, and longer flights raise the risk.

2. Her two moves
Heels up with toes on the floor, then switch. It works the calf muscles that push blood back up. And she stands and walks the aisle every couple of hours. She said passengers apologize for "getting in the way." She tells them not to.

3. The signs she watches for
Swelling, pain or warmth in one leg. Trouble breathing or chest pain means get help immediately. And if you've had clots before, or take hormones, ask your doctor before you fly.

Mine was the shock. I'd flown for 20 years and thought sleeping through it was the smart play.

(This is general information, not medical advice. Ask your doctor about your own risk.)
