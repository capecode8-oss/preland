Most of us know our mother's birthday. Almost none of us know the age she went through menopause.

That one number is worth more than you'd think. Researchers have been comparing mothers' and daughters' menopause timing for decades, and the pattern keeps showing up.

What the studies found:

1. Early runs in families
In one study, women with premature or early menopause had mothers who went through it at about 43.8 and 45.4 years, compared with 48.4 for women with normal timing.

2. The odds were much higher
The odds of early or premature menopause were about 6 times higher if the mother had early menopause.

3. It's partly genetic
A study of mother-daughter pairs estimated that about 44% of the timing is heritable. Treat that number as an estimate. It comes from a small study.

The honest limits: this is about patterns, not prediction. Plenty of women go through menopause at a very different age from their mothers, and the exact mechanism isn't known.

(Sources: studies on maternal age at menopause published in Menopause and Fertility and Sterility.)

Here's what I'd do. Call your mother, or your aunt, or your grandmother if she's still here. Ask three things: when her periods stopped, what she noticed in the years before, and whether anything surprised her. Write the answers down.
