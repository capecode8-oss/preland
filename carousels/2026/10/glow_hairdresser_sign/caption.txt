She never says it to clients. She only said it to me because I was the last appointment of the day.

Eleven years in her chair. I was 49, tired, half asleep under the dryer, when I asked what the strangest part of her job was. She laughed and said, "I can tell who's grieving, who's exhausted, and who's hiding something, just from the back of her head."

I figured it was a line. It wasn't.

Here's what she told me, in her words.

1. Hair remembers
Hair shows what happened two or three months ago. A rough stretch, little sleep, skipped meals, a crash diet. Women come in saying "my hair just started falling out." It didn't just start. It started in the summer.

2. The part tells the truth
When the part gets wider or the ponytail gets thinner, she notices first. Dry, snapping hair is the other one. She doesn't say it.

3. One gentle question
"How have the last few months been?" She says almost every woman tears up. Nobody had asked.

I went home and thought about the last six months. A move. My dad's surgery. Five hours of sleep. I'd blamed the shampoo.

Not every change means something serious, but mention a sudden one to your doctor. The point stuck: my body had been sending me notes in plain sight, and I'd been too busy to read them.

The biggest thing I took from that chair? Taking care of yourself later in life isn't starting too late. Sometimes it's the first time you finally put yourself on the list.
