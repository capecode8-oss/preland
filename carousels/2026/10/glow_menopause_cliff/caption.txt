The word "cliff" got the clicks. The details are more interesting.

On October 12, 2026, researchers from the Weizmann Institute published a study in Nature Medicine on what happens in the body around menopause. They used a computational method on more than 300 million lab tests from over a million women in the US and Israel, from 1999 to 2024.

What they found:

1. Many tests changed abruptly
In the US data, 52 of 90 lab tests, including bone, liver, kidney, blood chemistry and blood fat markers, showed a statistically significant abrupt change.

2. Hormones moved early
Three sex hormones changed more than a decade before the final menstrual period.

3. Hormone therapy was linked to smaller changes
This was observational and needs confirming.

The pushback: Martha Hickey, who heads menopause services and wasn't involved, called the "cliff-like" framing unnecessarily alarming and said it isn't a scientific or medical term. She noted that the established SWAN study suggests these changes are gradual.

The honest limits: this was retrospective lab data, menopause timing was partly self-reported, and experts disagree on how to read it.

(Sources: Nature Medicine, October 2026, as reported by Newsweek, ThePrint and Nature Asia.)

Whichever side is right, change may start years earlier than we expect. Ask your doctor about baseline blood pressure, cholesterol and blood sugar in your forties.
