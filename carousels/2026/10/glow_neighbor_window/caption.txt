I thought she was stretching. She was taking attendance.

My neighbor is 84 and has lived next door for thirty years. Every morning at 7:10, she stands barefoot at her kitchen window with one hand on the counter and her eyes closed. For exactly 90 seconds. I could see it from my driveway.

After a week I couldn't stand it and I knocked.

"I'm taking attendance," she said.

Here's what she told me.

1. Head, neck, stomach, knees
She goes down the list, one part at a time. One question for each: how are you today?

2. She listens
No judgment. No fix. She just listens for the answer. If the answer is "worse than yesterday," she writes it on a pad by the stove.

3. She keeps the pad
When something's off for three days, she calls her doctor. She said she's never once had to guess what to say at the appointment.

"My body talks," she said. "It's quieter at 84, but it still talks. Most people never stop to hear it."

I went home and stood in my own kitchen. Stomach: not great. For about a year. I'd been calling it "stress."

I canceled my plans for the day and booked the appointment I'd been putting off.

The biggest thing I've learned? Taking care of yourself later in life isn't "starting too late." Sometimes it's the first time you finally put yourself on the list.
