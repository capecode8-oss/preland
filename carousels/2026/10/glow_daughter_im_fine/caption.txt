She wasn't angry. That's what made it so hard to hear.

My daughter used to call every Sunday at 4. For six years, it was the same: "How are you, Mom?" "I'm fine, honey." Then she'd tell me about her week.

When the calls stopped, I told myself she was busy. After a month I called her. She answered on the first ring, and she said it gently.

Here's what she told me.

1. "I can hear it's not true"
She'd stopped asking because "I'm fine" came out too fast. She felt shut out of my life, and she was right.

2. What I'd been hiding
Bad sleep. Aching knees. A tired I couldn't shake. I didn't want to worry her. I'd been protecting her from the one thing she'd have wanted to know.

3. One true thing per call
We made a deal. Every Sunday I say one real thing. "Knees hurt today." "Slept badly." She said it was the best call we'd had in years.

When I told a friend about it, she said, "At your age, you shouldn't burden your kids." I almost believed her.

But being honest isn't a burden. It's the door.

She cried a little on the phone. So did I. Six years of "fine" had built a wall neither of us meant to build.

The biggest thing I've learned? Taking care of yourself later in life isn't "starting too late." Sometimes it's simply the first time you finally put yourself on the list.

I gave my family everything and kept nothing for my own body. When I finally listened to it, I wrote down what it had been telling me for years, and put it in one place: four short books (The Belly Fat Lie, Aging Faster Than You Should, The Caffeine Trap, The 3AM Signal) and four tools.
