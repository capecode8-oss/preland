My grandmother would have smelled the milk. I read the date and threw it out.

Last month I threw out a half gallon, a carton of eggs and a yogurt in one week. All of it was "expired" by one day. I got curious and looked up what the dates actually mean.

Here's what the USDA says, in plain words.

1. Dates aren't safety dates
Except for infant formula, the dates on food are not required by federal law and are not a safety deadline. "Best if used by" means quality. "Sell by" means the store should move it.

2. Spoilage is what matters
Properly stored food that shows no spoilage is generally safe past the date. Mold, a sour smell or a changed texture means toss it.

3. The cost
The FDA has estimated that confusion about these dates accounts for about 20% of consumer food waste.

I told my husband I'd been throwing out $20 of groceries a week for years. He laughed, then did the math.

When I mentioned it to my sister, she said, "I'd never trust my nose." But our grandmothers did, every day of their lives.

Keep one caution in mind: your nose can't catch everything. Keep your fridge at 40°F or below, and when you're in doubt about leftovers, deli meat or anything for a baby or a pregnant woman, throw it out.
