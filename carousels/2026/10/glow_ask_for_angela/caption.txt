My niece told me about a night out that went wrong. A man wouldn't take no for an answer. She remembered a poster in the restroom and asked the bartender one sentence: "Is Angela working tonight?"

Here's how "Ask for Angela" works, based on police and local council pages.

1. The request
You ask a bartender, bouncer or staff member: "Is Angela working?" It's a quiet signal that you need help, with no explanation.

2. The response
Trained staff can take you to a safe space, call a taxi, help you reach a friend, ask the other person to leave, or call security or police.

3. The catch
It only works at venues that joined and trained their staff. It began in Lincolnshire, England, in 2016 and spread to Ireland and Canada. I couldn't confirm a US program, so look for a poster first.

My niece was walked to the back office and a taxi came ten minutes later.

When she told her mother, her mother said, "I wish we'd had something like that." Many women say this.

A code word is a good idea. An even better one is a plan before the night starts.

Her advice now: look at the restroom wall for the poster when you arrive. Some venues have one, some don't. It takes ten seconds to check, and it's worth knowing before you need it.
