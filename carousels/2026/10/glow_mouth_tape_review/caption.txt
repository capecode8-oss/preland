The scary part isn't that it doesn't work. It's what it can hide.

Mouth taping went viral as a free way to sleep better. A 2025 systematic review gathered every published study on it: 10 studies, 213 people in total.

What the reviewers found:

1. Weak evidence
Only 2 of the 10 studies suggested a possible benefit, and those were in people with mild sleep apnea. The others showed no difference.

2. A real risk
Four studies warned of a serious risk of choking if mouth breathing comes from a blocked nose. Taped lips can also make it harder to clear vomit or reflux.

3. It can mask a problem
People drawn to mouth taping often snore or wake unrefreshed. Those can be signs of undiagnosed sleep apnea.

The reviewers described a potentially serious risk of harm for people trying it indiscriminately.

(Source: a 2025 systematic review in PLOS ONE, as reported by HealthDay.)

If you snore, stop breathing at night, or wake up tired, don't tape anything. See a doctor and ask about a sleep test.

A safer first step costs nothing: for a week, note whether you snore, wake with a dry mouth or a headache, or feel tired no matter how long you stay in bed. Those notes are what a sleep doctor actually asks about, and they matter more than any trend.
