The sign in the photo was adorable. The police officer reading it saw something else.

A friend of mine, Renee, was scrolling through the back-to-school posts in her feed when she noticed something. Every sign had the same ingredients: name, age, grade, school, teacher. She'd posted one herself.

Police departments have been repeating the same advice, so I wrote it down.

1. Names, age and school
Keep the full name, age and school off the photo. A logo on a shirt or a banner behind them counts.

2. Location
Turn off tags. Don't post in real time from the bus stop or the school door.

3. Backgrounds and favorites
House numbers, street signs and license plates give away where you live. Favorites, like a teacher's name or a pet's name, help strangers pretend to know your child.

Police also suggest a simple test: would you hand a printed copy to a stranger on the street?

Renee deleted three posts and replaced the sign with a plain "First Day of School."

I made a note to talk to my own kids, too.

We protect what we love. And we protect our kids much better than we protect ourselves.

Most of what a stranger can learn is already sitting in plain sight, in captions and backgrounds. A few seconds of checking before you post is enough, and it works for your own photos too.
