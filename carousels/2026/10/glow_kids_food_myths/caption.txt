Ask a grandmother what scares her about children's food, and the list gets long. A pediatrician in Seoul says most of it shouldn't.

Early puberty is a growing worry in South Korea. The number of children and teens diagnosed rose about 72% between 2019 and 2023, from roughly 109,000 to 187,000. Parents look for something to blame, and foods take the fall: milk, eggs, even blueberries.

What Dr. Choi Jung-eun, a pediatrics professor at Ewha Womans University Mokdong Hospital, told The Korea Times:

1. The food fears are myths
Milk, eggs and blueberries do not have a significant impact on early puberty.

2. What she recommends
A varied diet, fewer instant and frozen foods, regular exercise and keeping weight in check.

3. Screens matter, indirectly
She says screen time doesn't directly cause early puberty but may contribute through weight gain.

Other research fits. A 2012 Hong Kong study found cow's milk doesn't appear to raise the risk. A large 2025 Korean study suggested babies who were breastfed were less likely to go through early puberty.

(Sources: The Korea Times, August 2025; Live Science on the 2012 Hong Kong study; JAMA Network Open, 2025.)

If you're worried about a child, talk to their pediatrician. This is general information, not medical advice.
