My mother rinsed her chicken in the sink until the day she died. She called it "being careful."

I did it too for thirty years. Then a food safety class changed what I do in my kitchen.

Here's what the CDC and USDA say, in plain words.

1. Washing spreads it
Water splashes bacteria like Salmonella off raw chicken, and the USDA says it can land in a 2 to 3 foot radius: your counter, your dish towel, the sponge, a salad bowl.

2. Their own test
In a USDA study, 60% of people who washed raw poultry had bacteria in the sink afterward. About a quarter transferred it to lettuce sitting nearby.

3. What works instead
Don't rinse. Pat dry with a paper towel. Cook to 165°F, which kills the germs. Then wipe your counter with hot soapy water and sanitize it.

When I told my sister, she said, "But that's how Mom taught us." Exactly. Nobody ever questioned it.

I stood in my kitchen holding a wet piece of chicken, and I realized I'd been doing something for thirty years because no one asked why.

I don't think I'm the only one running on habits I never checked.

One more tip from the same class: use separate cutting boards for raw meat and vegetables, and wash your hands for 20 seconds with soap after touching raw chicken. Small changes, no special equipment, and they cost nothing.
