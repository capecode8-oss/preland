Her doctor didn't ask about her diet. She asked what time the lights went out.

She was 46. For six months she'd done everything by the book. Same diet. Same walks. She had raised three kids mostly on her own, and it was finally her turn to put herself on the list.

When she told her friends she was frustrated about the belly that wouldn't move, one of them said, "At your age? That's just what happens."

She almost believed it.

Then her doctor named three things.

1. Sleep is not a luxury
Five hours a night felt normal to her. Short sleep can raise hunger and stress hormones. Both can push the body to hold fat around the middle. After 40, hormones are already shifting, so it stacks up.

2. The phone in the bed
Scrolling until 1AM isn't rest. The light and the noise keep the brain switched on. Her doctor asked for one change: phone out of the bedroom.

3. Forty-five minutes
Not an hour. Not a new routine. Bed forty-five minutes earlier, and a note each morning about how she felt.

Two months later, her waistband felt different. Not magic. Just nights.

The biggest thing she took from it? Taking care of yourself after 40 isn't starting too late. Sometimes it's the first time you finally put yourself on the list.
