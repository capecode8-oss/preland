He had my bag before I'd said yes.

That's how fast it happens. Arrivals hall, jet lag, and a friendly man in a clean jacket saying, "Taxi, ma'am? Good price."

I'd been awake for twenty hours. I followed him to a car with nothing on the roof, no meter, and a back seat that smelled like someone else's cigarettes.

Nothing happened. Not the way you imagine. I paid three times the normal fare and he dropped me at the right hotel. A story for dinner.

But on that ride I realized something. If he had taken a different turn, nobody on earth knew where I was. No receipt. No driver ID. No record that I'd gotten into that car at all.

A hotel doorman told me the next morning that official taxis don't approach you inside the terminal. The ones who do are working for themselves.

Here's what I do before every flight now.

I screenshot the official taxi stand or ride-app pickup spot for that airport, because I'll be too tired to think when I land.

I use the app, and I match the plate and the driver's photo to the car before I open the door.

I text the plate and the route to someone. It takes five seconds and changes the whole ride.

I say no to anyone who offers before I ask. A polite no thank you, and I keep walking. They stop following.

Most of these rides end fine. I'm not telling you they don't. I'm telling you what the other ones have in common: nobody knew.
