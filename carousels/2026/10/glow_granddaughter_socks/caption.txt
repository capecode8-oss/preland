She went back to her cereal like she'd asked about the weather. I stood there with one sock on.

I'm 61. My granddaughter Lily is six, and she misses nothing. That Saturday she looked up from her bowl and asked, "Grandma, why do you always sit down to put on your socks?"

I opened my mouth to say I don't. I do. I've done it for two years and called it "being careful."

Here's what I did next.

1. I counted
Sitting for socks. Holding the counter to stand up. Taking stairs one at a time. Three things in one morning I'd never put together.

2. I tried the one-leg test
Brushing my teeth, I stood on one leg for ten seconds. Right side fine. Left side, I wobbled by five. It isn't a diagnosis, but it's information.

3. I told my doctor, with examples
Not "I feel old." Specific mornings. She checked my balance and gave me a few easy exercises. I do them while the coffee brews. If you ever feel dizzy or unsteady, mention it to your doctor.

When I told a friend, she said, "At your age, balance goes." I almost believed her.

But that wasn't the whole story.

Lily's next question was even better. "Does it hurt?" No, baby. It just makes me feel older than I am. She thought about it and said, "Then why not fix it like my bike?" Out of the mouths of six-year-olds.

The biggest thing I've learned? Taking care of yourself later in life isn't "starting too late." Sometimes it's simply the first time you finally put yourself on the list.
