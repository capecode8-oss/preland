The first thing the driver said when she asked was nothing. That's what saved her.

My neighbor's 16-year-old, Maya, ordered her first ride home from a friend's house. The app showed one car. A different car pulled up, with the driver saying, "Maya?" She hesitated. Her mom had given her one rule.

Here's what her mom taught her, based on official safety tips for teen riders.

1. Check before you open the door
Plate, driver's name and photo in the app. If any of the three don't match, don't get in. Uber's own teen tips say to ask the driver's name too.

2. Ask: "Who are you here to pick up?"
Safety guides suggest this one. The real driver says the name from the app. It isn't an official Uber line, but it's easy to teach.

3. Use the PIN and the back seat
Uber's teen accounts have a PIN the rider gives the driver before the trip starts. Sit in the back. Share the trip with a parent.

Maya asked the question. The man looked away and drove off. Her real ride arrived six minutes later.

When her mom told other parents, one said, "She's old enough to handle it." Her mom said, "Handling it is knowing what to ask."
