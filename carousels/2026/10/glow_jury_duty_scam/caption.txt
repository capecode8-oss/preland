The caller always stays on the line. That's not an accident.

Police departments in several cities have warned about a jury duty scam again this month. A caller says you missed jury duty, there's a warrant, and you can avoid arrest by paying a fine right now.

How it works:

1. They pose as court staff or officers
Sheriff's deputies, court clerks, even federal officers.

2. They demand payment fast
Gift cards, payment apps, cryptocurrency or cash. Some ask you to photograph the cards, or mail them to a clerk's office to seem legitimate.

3. They keep you on the phone
If you stay on the line, you can't stop and think, and you can't call anyone.

What real courts do: they send jury summonses by mail. They don't demand payment by phone, email, gift card or cryptocurrency. Your caller ID may show a real courthouse number, but scammers can fake it.

(Sources: county clerk and state consumer-protection offices in Wisconsin and Nebraska; local police alerts.)

If you get the call: hang up, don't pay anything, and call the court clerk using the number from the court's official website. If you've already paid, contact your bank, payment app or the gift card company immediately, and report it at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.
