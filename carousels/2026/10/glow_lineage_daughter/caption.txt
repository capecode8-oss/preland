Ask a mother who her family's story continues through, and most will say her son, because he carries the name. Biology tells a quieter story.

Inside almost every cell in your body are mitochondria, tiny structures that turn food into energy. They carry their own DNA, and you inherited it from your mother, not your father.

Here's how the chain works:

1. A mother passes it to every child
Sons and daughters both receive their mother's mitochondrial DNA.

2. Only daughters pass it on
A son carries it his whole life, but he doesn't pass it to his children. A daughter does.

3. It goes back about 200,000 years
Geneticists trace everyone alive today to one woman, nicknamed Mitochondrial Eve. Estimates put her at roughly 200,000 years ago. She wasn't the only woman alive then. Hers is simply the one line that never broke.

To be fair, fathers have their own thread: the Y chromosome passes from father to son. Each line tells half the story. The mother's line is the one that runs through the mitochondria.

(Sources: ScienceDaily and Cornell University coverage of mitochondrial Eve research; The Scientist on maternal inheritance.)
