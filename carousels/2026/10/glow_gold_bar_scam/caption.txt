The scariest part is how calm the caller sounds.

Pennsylvania State Police issued a warning about a scam that has taken an estimated $6.5 million. Victims across several states, including Maryland and Washington, have been hit. Two people were arrested after a traffic stop on Interstate 80 on September 10, 2026.

How it works, according to police:

1. It starts with fear
A computer pop-up or a call from someone posing as a federal agent says your identity or bank accounts are tied to a crime.

2. Then comes the ask
You're told to withdraw cash, buy gold, and hand it to a courier, supposedly to protect it.

3. Then it's gone
The "courier" is part of the scam. Once the gold or cash is handed over, it's gone.

What police say every time: no legitimate law enforcement officer will tell you to buy gold or give cash or gold to a courier. Montgomery County police describe the calls as run by organized call centers overseas, and police in Shelton, Connecticut say seniors are being targeted.

(Sources: Pennsylvania State Police, Shelton CT police, Montgomery County MD police.)

If this happens to someone you love: tell them to hang up, call a family member, and report it at ReportFraud.ftc.gov. If gold or cash has already been handed over, call local police right away.
