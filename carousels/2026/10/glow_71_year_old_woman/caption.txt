She was the quietest person at the table. I'd almost forgotten she was there.

We were at a family dinner, and I turned to her on a whim. She's 71. I asked what she'd say to the woman she was at 45. I expected something gentle. Something like "enjoy every moment."

She put her coffee down and said she wouldn't say that at all.

What she told me:

1. Stop waiting for permission
"Nobody is coming to tell you it's your turn."

2. Care for yourself before it's an emergency
She spent her forties looking after everyone else. Her own checkup waited until she was 52.

3. Start small
She doesn't believe in big overhauls. Book the appointment. Walk after dinner. Say no once a week. Start with one.

She wasn't telling me to overhaul my life. She was telling me to stop treating my own needs as the thing that can always wait until the house is quiet, the kids are settled and the calendar clears. It never clears.

I had nothing to say. She said it without any bitterness, which is exactly why it hit so hard.

Here's what stayed with me. Most of the women I know are waiting for a sign, an invitation, or a calmer season to take care of themselves. That calmer season doesn't arrive. Taking care of yourself later in life isn't starting too late. Sometimes it's simply the first time you put yourself on the list.

I've been waiting for someone to tell me it's my turn. Nobody ever will.
