The test takes under two minutes. No gym. No equipment. Just a floor.

Researchers in Brazil asked 2,002 adults aged 51 to 80 to sit down on the floor and stand back up. They scored it out of 10. Five points for sitting, five for rising, and you lose a point for every time you use a hand, a knee or an arm. Then they followed everyone for about six years.

What they found:

1. Each point mattered
Every point lost was linked to a 21% higher risk of dying from any cause.

2. The low end was stark
People who scored 0 to 3 had a risk 5 to 6 times higher than people who scored 8 to 10.

3. It isn't magic
It's an observational study. The authors suggest it reflects flexibility and balance, which we can all work on.

(Source: the European Society of Cardiology press release on the 2012 study by Brito and colleagues.)

If you score low, that's not a verdict. It's information. Talk to your doctor about where to start, and don't try anything that hurts.

If you try it today, do it on a carpet or a mat, near a sofa or a wall you can reach, and stop if anything hurts. The point isn't to win. It's to get an honest starting score you can compare in a month.
