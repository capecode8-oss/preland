Women in their 40s keep being told the same thing about sleep: it's stress. It isn't only stress.

A narrative review of sleep and perimenopause found that about 56% of perimenopausal women report broken sleep, compared with about 32% of women the same age who haven't started the transition.

What the research says:

1. Night sweats are a big part
Hot flashes at night are linked to a large share of night awakenings, but estimates vary a lot, from about a quarter to more than two thirds.

2. They're not the whole story
In one study, nighttime hot flashes explained only about 27% of time awake in the night. Sleep can break up without them.

3. Other suspects
Researchers name changes in the body clock, lower melatonin, aging itself, and mood.

The honest limits: most of these numbers come from self-reports, and results differ between groups of women. The studies I found didn't look at one exact hour like 3 AM.

(Source: "Sleep Disturbance and Perimenopause: A Narrative Review," 2024.)

What helps most: keep a simple log for two weeks. When you wake, what you ate, what you felt. Bring it to your doctor. It turns "I sleep badly" into something they can actually use.

One more reason to write it down: when you tell a doctor "I sleep badly," there's not much to work with. When you say "I wake around the same hour four nights out of seven," that's a pattern they can act on.
