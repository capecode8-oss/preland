Most of us finish dinner and head straight for the couch. This is why that may matter.

A 2022 review in Sports Medicine pulled together seven studies that compared sitting, standing and walking, and measured blood sugar and insulin.

What they found:

1. Walking beat sitting
Short light walks of about 2 to 5 minutes, repeated every 20 to 30 minutes, lowered blood sugar by about 17% on average.

2. Standing helped less
Standing breaks lowered blood sugar by about 9.5%, but didn't lower insulin. Walking improved both.

3. The rise and fall was gentler
After a short walk, blood sugar went up and down more gradually.

The honest limits: the breaks were spread through the day, not only after meals. These were seven small studies. And they measured blood sugar and insulin, not whether anyone developed diabetes.

(Source: Sports Medicine, 2022, as reported by CNN.)

If you have diabetes or take medication, ask your doctor before changing anything. For everyone else, a short easy walk after dinner is a low-risk thing to try.

Nobody in these studies was asked to run or sweat. The walks were slow and short, about the pace of clearing the table and wandering to the mailbox. If that sounds too small to matter, that's exactly why most of us skip it.
