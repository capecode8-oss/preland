The ATM in Rome didn't look suspicious. It looked like every ATM I'd ever used.

That's the part that still bothers me.

It was 11pm and I needed cash for a taxi. My card went in, the PIN worked, and the machine asked a friendly question: pay in dollars or euros?

Dollars. Obviously. I'm American. I know what a dollar is worth.

I pressed it without reading anything else on the screen.

What I didn't know is that when you choose your home currency at a foreign ATM, the machine decides the exchange rate. Not your bank. It's called dynamic currency conversion, and the rate is usually worse. On my $600 withdrawal, I lost about $61 for pressing the button that looked easier.

I found out two days later, reading my statement at breakfast next to a hotel employee who didn't look surprised at all. She said it's one of the first things she warns guests about.

Here's what I do now.

Always choose the local currency. Euros in Europe, pesos in Mexico, yen in Japan. Say no to anything that says "convert for you" or "guaranteed rate."

Decline it even when the screen makes it sound like a favor. It's the same screen in almost every country.

Take out larger amounts less often. A flat bank fee hurts less when you pay it once.

Check the receipt before you leave the machine. If it shows an exchange rate, your bank didn't set it.

A tiny decision, made in two seconds, at night. That's exactly why it works on people.
