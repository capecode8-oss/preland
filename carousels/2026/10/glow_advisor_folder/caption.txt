She didn't say "crypto." She didn't say "real estate." She said, "How are you sleeping?"

I'd been with my advisor for fifteen years. Every February we sat in the same glass office and she showed me a chart going up. I was 52 and proud of that chart.

Then she closed the folder and asked me a question that had nothing to do with money.

Here's what she told me.

1. The plan only works if you're there
She said she's watched clients retire at 65 with every box checked, then spend the first years in doctors' waiting rooms instead of at the lake house.

2. Two questions at every review
How are you sleeping? When was your last full check-up? She said most people answer the first with a shrug and the second with "I'll get to it."

3. Treat your body like an asset
She put it simply. You'd never skip a year of checking your accounts. Why skip a year of checking the thing that earns the money?

I went home and counted. My last full check-up was four years ago. I'd been "getting to it."

When I told a friend I was finally making appointments, she said, "At your age, what are they going to find?" I almost believed her.

But I booked it anyway.

The biggest thing I've learned? Taking care of yourself later in life isn't "starting too late." Sometimes it's simply the first time you finally put yourself on the list.
