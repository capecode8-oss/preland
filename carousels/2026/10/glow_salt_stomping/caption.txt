The salt isn't the point. A doctor said the point is what you do while you stand there.

"Salt stomping" took over social feeds this summer. You pour coarse sea salt or Himalayan salt into a tray, stand on it barefoot for five to ten minutes, and shift your weight. Videos say it lowers stress, helps circulation and gets you to sleep.

What the experts told reporters:

1. Little evidence
One clinician said there's very little scientific evidence that standing on dry salt lowers cortisol, regulates the nervous system, improves circulation or helps you sleep.

2. No absorption
The skin on your feet doesn't absorb minerals from dry salt in meaningful amounts.

3. The ritual may be the benefit
One physician said the value lies in the pause: a quiet "grounding" moment before bed, not the salt.

(Sources: Stylist and The Nod, July 2026.)

That matches what many of us already feel: five minutes without a phone, without a to-do list, feet on the floor, breathing slowly. It costs nothing. You don't need the salt.

If you already like the salt tray, keep it. I didn't see anyone in the coverage call it dangerous. Just don't expect the salt itself to fix your sleep, and don't skip a real conversation with your doctor if nights stay bad for weeks.
