The instructor didn't start with a bunker. She started with a door.

My phone buzzed at a friend's kitchen table last spring with an emergency test alert. Everyone laughed. Then she asked us one question: "What would you do in the first minute if it were real?" Nobody had an answer. Mine was "run to the car."

That's the instinct she said gets people hurt. Three things, in order.

1. Get inside
Nearest building, any building. Then go to the middle or below ground, away from outer walls and windows. Brick and concrete protect you far better than wood.

2. Stay inside
Plan on 24 hours unless officials say otherwise. Everyone wants to find their family right away. Don't. Reunite later.

3. Stay tuned
Phones and internet may be down. A battery or hand-crank radio keeps working, and it will tell you when it's safe to leave.

(Source for all three: the U.S. government's Ready.gov guidance.)

Walking home I realized something that bothered me. I knew my first 60 seconds for an alert I'll probably never see. I had no plan at all for the warnings my body sends me every week.

We know how to spot a scam. We know where the exit is on a plane. Almost nobody teaches us how to read our own body's warning signs.
