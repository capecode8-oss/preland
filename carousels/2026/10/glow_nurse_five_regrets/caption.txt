She didn't write it for a study. She wrote it because she kept hearing the same thing.

Bronnie Ware, an Australian palliative care nurse, spent years caring for people in their last weeks. She started writing down what they said they regretted most, and published it as a blog post. It was read by more than three million people in its first year and became a memoir, published in 2012.

The five regrets she described:

1. "I wish I'd had the courage to live a life true to myself, not the life others expected of me."

2. "I wish I hadn't worked so hard."

3. "I wish I'd had the courage to express my feelings."

4. "I wish I had stayed in touch with my friends."

5. "I wish I had let myself be happier."

To be clear: this is one nurse's account of what patients told her. It isn't a scientific study. It's a list that moved millions of people because so many of us recognize ourselves in it.

(Source: Bronnie Ware, The Top Five Regrets of the Dying, 2012, and her original blog post.)

Read the list again and notice which one stings. For many women I know it's the first and the last: living for other people's expectations, and putting happiness on hold.
