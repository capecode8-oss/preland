Try it before you read on. Stand barefoot, lift one foot, count to ten.

That's the whole test. Researchers in Brazil used it on 1,702 adults aged 51 to 75 and then followed them for years. The people who couldn't hold it for 10 seconds had an 84% higher risk of death from any cause, even after adjusting for age, sex and existing conditions.

Three things to know:

1. It gets harder with age
About 95% of people aged 51 to 55 could do it. By 61 to 65 it was 82%. By 71 to 75 only 46% could.

2. It's a link, not a cause
The study only watched people. It can't say that failing the test causes anything. It also had no data on falls, activity, diet or medications.

3. The authors suggest using it at checkups
They said it's quick, free and could be part of routine health checks for older adults.

If you try it, stand near a counter or wall you can grab, and skip it if you feel dizzy or have been told to avoid balance exercises. Try both legs, and note which side is harder.

(Sources: the British Journal of Sports Medicine, 2022, as reported by the University of Eastern Finland and the American College of Physicians.)

If you can't hold it, don't panic. Ask your doctor about your balance and strength. Both can be trained at almost any age.
