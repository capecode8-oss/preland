If the ringing changes when you move your jaw, tell your doctor. It helps them more than you'd think.

Ringing in the ears is one of those things people quietly live with for years. Most of us assume the problem is the ear. For a lot of people, researchers say, it isn't only the ear.

What the studies found:

1. Jaw movements changed it
In a Korean study of 163 patients (217 ears), moving or clenching the jaw changed the loudness in 57.6% of ears. Opening and clenching most often made it louder.

2. Neck and jaw maneuvers too
A clinic study of 81 patients found that 61.7% saw a change after one or more neck or jaw movements.

3. It has a name
Doctors call this somatic tinnitus: ringing that is influenced by the muscles and joints of the neck and jaw.

The honest limits: most of these studies are small or based on clinic records. They show that the link is real for many people. They don't prove how common it is, and I couldn't find large trials showing that treating the neck or jaw makes the ringing go away.

(Sources: a Korean study of 163 patients on somatic testing of tinnitus, and a clinic cohort of 81 patients, as summarized in published research on somatosensory tinnitus.)

See a doctor first if the ringing came on suddenly, is in one ear only, comes with hearing loss or dizziness, or pulses with your heartbeat. Those need to be checked, not guessed at.
