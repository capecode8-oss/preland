It tasted the same. That was the whole plan.

For decades, McDonald's french fries were cooked in a blend that was mostly beef tallow. Tallow has a high smoke point, and at the time it was cheaper than vegetable oil. It's a big part of why the fries tasted the way people remember.

What happened next:

1. 1990: the switch
McDonald's announced it would move to 100% vegetable oil. Several accounts link the change to pressure from health advocates, though the company has never said so definitively.

2. The taste problem
Without tallow, the fries tasted different. The company added natural beef flavoring to the oil to recover some of the original taste.

3. 2007: another change
The company moved again, to a trans-fat-free cooking oil.

The point isn't about one restaurant. It's that a recipe can change while the logo, the box and the memory all stay the same. Most of us checked labels once, years ago, and never looked again.

(Sources: Food Republic, Chowhound, The Takeout and Tasting Table histories of the McDonald's fry. Motives and exact dates vary by account.)
