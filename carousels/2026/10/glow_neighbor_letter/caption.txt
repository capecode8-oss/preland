I thought a note would say sorry. This one said thank you, and I've been thinking about it for a month.

We had a beagle named Bruno who barked at 5 AM, at the paperboy, at the wind. Our neighbor Walter, in his late 70s, never said a word. He waved over the fence every morning, like the barking was a greeting.

When Walter moved to be near his daughter, he left us a letter.

1. The alarm clock
He wrote that he took a heart pill at 5:15 every morning. Bruno's barking reminded him. He'd stopped setting an alarm years ago.

2. The silence
He lived alone. He never asked for help because he never wanted to be the neighbor who needed it.

3. The thank-you
"You were the only one who made noise on my street. It meant someone was alive out there."

I read it standing at the mailbox, and I realized I'd never once asked Walter how he was.

I walked down our street that week and knocked on six doors. Two people said, almost the same words: "Nobody asked."

Everyone has a body that's saying something. The loud ones get attention. The quiet ones get a wave over the fence.

Walter sends us a postcard every winter now. Last one said, "Bruno sounds old." He's right. Bruno is 14. I read it to him, and he wagged. That's the whole story. Be the one who knocks.

We check labels, bills and fine print. I started reading what my body had been trying to tell me, and put it in one place: four short books (The Belly Fat Lie, Aging Faster Than You Should, The Caffeine Trap, The 3AM Signal) and four tools. That's the Health Bundle.
