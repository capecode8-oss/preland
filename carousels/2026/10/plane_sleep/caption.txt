The scary part isn't the sleep. It's four hours of sitting completely still.

In 2007 the World Health Organization released results of its WRIGHT study on travel and blood clots. It found the risk of a clot in a vein roughly doubles after travel of four hours or more. WHO described the absolute risk for a single long trip as low, about 1 in 6,000 in that report. Another summary says about 1 in 4,600, so it varies by source. The extra risk stays up for roughly four weeks.

WHO pointed to one mechanism: blood slowing down in the veins of the legs when you stay immobile for hours. Risk goes higher with things like obesity, being very tall, oral contraceptives, inherited clotting disorders, recent surgery, hormone use, and a past clot.

What the sources I checked recommend:

1. Move your legs
Get up and walk at least every two to three hours. An aisle seat makes that easier.

2. Can't get up? Do seat exercises
Lift your heels with your toes on the floor, then lift your toes with your heels down. Tighten and release your leg muscles.

3. Wear loose clothing
If you're at higher risk, ask your doctor about compression stockings before a long trip.

4. Know the warning signs
Leg pain, tenderness, swelling, unusual warmth, or redness. Chest pain, trouble breathing, or coughing up blood needs medical care right away.

5. Keep your seat belt on when you sleep
Travel pros say to buckle over your blanket so the crew can see it and doesn't have to wake you.

Honest note: the studies measured sitting still, not a sleeping position. I found no study showing that sleeping itself changes the risk, and for most people the risk on any one flight is small.
