The internet says it's illegal to die in this Norwegian town. That's not true.

The real rule is about burial. Longyearbyen, on the Arctic island of Svalbard, stopped burying people decades ago. The cemetery is closed.

The reason is the ground. It's permafrost. It stays frozen all year, so bodies buried there don't break down the way they do almost anywhere else.

The explanation you'll see repeated is about germs. Researchers have long wondered whether viruses and bacteria, even the one behind the 1918 flu, could survive in frozen remains. The details differ from source to source, so treat that part as the commonly told reason.

What people can count on is the plan for the very ill. Reports say they're usually flown to a hospital on the mainland.

One small town. One rule. And a reason hiding in the ground.
