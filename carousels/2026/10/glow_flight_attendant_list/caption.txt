She never raised her voice. That's how I knew she meant every word.

On a flight to Lisbon I asked the purser what drives crew up the wall. Twenty-eight years in the aisle. She didn't have to think.

Here's her list.

1. Poking, tapping, snapping fingers
Number one, she said. A calm "excuse me" goes a long way.

2. Ignoring the seatbelt sign
Standing up while it's lit. It isn't about rules. A bump can throw a person into a seat arm.

3. Feet on the seat or the wall
Think of everyone who touches that seat next.

Then she lowered her voice.

"That's the annoying list," she said. "But it's not the one that worries me. The passenger who worries me never presses the call button. Quiet. Pale. 'I'm fine.'"

She told me crew are trained to notice color, breathing and confusion. She has walked past a woman saying "I'm fine" more than once and come back ten minutes later to a gray face. "I'd rather be called for nothing a hundred times than miss it once."

And what to do instead? Press the button early. "I don't feel right" is a complete sentence. Nobody on that plane will think you're rude.

I've said "I'm fine" on the ground for years too. A plane doesn't create that habit. It just shows it.

The biggest thing I've learned? Taking care of yourself later in life isn't "starting too late." Sometimes it's simply the first time you finally put yourself on the list.

Professionals notice what the rest of us miss. I started noticing what my body had been telling me, and wrote it all down in one place: four short books (The Belly Fat Lie, Aging Faster Than You Should, The Caffeine Trap, The 3AM Signal) and four tools. That's the Health Bundle.
