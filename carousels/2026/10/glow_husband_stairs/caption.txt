It wasn't the stairs. It was that someone had been watching me for a year, and I hadn't.

Twenty-seven years. We were eating pasta on a Tuesday when my husband said, almost lightly, "When did you stop taking the stairs two at a time?"

I laughed. Then I couldn't stop thinking about it.

Here's what came out over the next few days.

1. What he'd seen
I paused halfway up. I held the rail. I said "just thinking" when I was catching my breath. He had noticed all of it and kept quiet because he didn't want to scare me.

2. What I'd said to myself
"It's age." That was my answer for everything. Tired, slower, foggy. My friends said it too. "At our age, what do you expect?"

3. What he said
"Maybe. Ask someone." It wasn't an argument. It was a door.

I booked the check-up, and I brought the list. I told the doctor everything he'd seen. It wasn't one thing, and a list gets a real answer where "I'm tired" gets a shrug.

I'm not saying the stairs meant something terrible. I'm saying someone saw me, and I'd stopped seeing myself first.

The biggest thing I've learned? Taking care of yourself later in life isn't "starting too late." Sometimes it's the first time you finally put yourself on the list.
