I bought a new mattress. Then a sleep mask. Then a $40 pillow spray that smelled like a spa and did nothing.

For a year I woke up at 3AM. Every night. Wide awake, heart a little fast, doing math about everything I'd ever worried about.

I raised my kids, ran a house and held down a job. Sleep was always the thing I'd fix "later." When I finally went to a doctor, I expected her to hand me a pill.

My friends said, "At your age, that's just normal." I almost believed them.

It wasn't.

Here's what my doctor actually asked me.

1. What time is your last coffee?
Mine was 2pm. She told me caffeine can stay in your body for six hours or more. A 2pm cup can still be working at 8pm. After 40, many women find it hits harder and sleep gets lighter.

2. Do you eat before your first cup?
I never did. She said coffee on an empty stomach can leave you wired all day, and wired at 8am can turn into awake at 3AM.

3. What happens at 3AM?
She asked me to write it down for two weeks. Time, how I felt, what I'd had the day before. A list gets you a real answer. "I'm tired" gets you a shrug.

By week two I was sleeping through the night five days out of seven. Not magic. Just timing.

The biggest thing I've learned? Waking up at 3AM doesn't mean something is wrong with you. Sometimes it's your body asking for a different morning.

I kept thinking it was just aging. The belly. The skin. The 3AM wake-ups. It wasn't aging. My body had been sending signals for years, and nobody taught me how to read them.
