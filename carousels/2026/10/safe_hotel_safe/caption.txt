The manager laughed when I asked who else could open my room safe.

Not in a mean way. In a "you really haven't thought about this" way.

I'd been putting my passport in hotel safes for years. Pick a code, hear the click, feel protected. I never once asked what happens when a guest forgets the code, or when something stays locked inside after checkout.

So I asked. He told me nearly every hotel safe has a master override. A manager code, or a key. The hotel needs a way in. That's a good reason for it to exist. It also means the safe is only as secure as the process and the people around it.

Here's what he said he would do in my place.

Photograph everything before you close the door. Passport, cash, jewelry, with the date visible. If something goes missing, a photo is the difference between a claim and an argument.

Ask the front desk if your safe keeps an audit log. Some models do. Many cheaper ones don't. You can't tell by looking at it.

Keep the one thing you can't replace on you. For me, that's the passport. A stolen passport abroad can turn into days of paperwork. A stolen watch is just a watch.

And carry a small cable lock. Ten or fifteen dollars. It turns a bag into something that doesn't walk out the door.

I stopped treating the safe like a vault. Now it's a speed bump. Useful. Not magic.
