It isn't the laundry. It's the water that leaves it.

In winter a lot of us dry clothes indoors: on the radiator, on a rack, over a door. It feels like the thrifty choice. But as clothes dry, the water in them has to go somewhere.

What I found in consumer and health coverage:

1. It adds up
Several sources say one load of laundry puts about 2 liters of water into your indoor air as it dries.

2. Damp invites mould
One article quoting experts says mould can spread when indoor humidity stays above about 60%.

3. Some people feel it more
Asthma charities say damp air and mould spores can trigger or worsen asthma in people sensitive to them. Dust mites also increase in humid conditions.

The honest limits: these figures come mostly from consumer and appliance-company articles and expert quotes, not from one big study. I couldn't confirm them against the World Health Organization's dampness and mould guidance, so treat the numbers as rough.

(Sources: Onmanorama, Meaco, Ebac and Quotezone coverage of indoor drying; asthma charity guidance quoted in those articles.)

What helps: open a window, use a dehumidifier if you have one, and dry outside when you can. If you have asthma, ask your doctor what to do about damp air in your home.
