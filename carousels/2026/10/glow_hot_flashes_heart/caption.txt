Nobody tells you hot flashes might be about more than comfort.

A big study called SWAN has followed American women through menopause for decades. In 2021, researchers led by Rebecca Thurston published a look at hot flashes and heart disease. They followed 3,083 women who were 42 to 52 at the start, for up to 22 years. None had heart disease when it began.

What they found:

1. Frequent hot flashes were linked to more heart events
Women who had hot flashes on at least 6 days in a 2-week period had a 51% higher risk of heart disease events than women who had none.

2. A few weren't
Women with hot flashes on 1 to 5 days showed no significant link.

3. It held after the usual suspects
The link remained after adjusting for traditional heart risk factors.

The honest limits: this is an observational study. It shows a link, not a cause. Hot flashes were self-reported. And the lead researcher noted we don't know whether treating hot flashes prevents heart disease. A few earlier studies didn't find the same link.

(Source: Thurston and colleagues, Journal of the American Heart Association, 2021.)

What I'd do: don't panic, and don't ignore it. At your next visit, mention the hot flashes and ask about your blood pressure and cholesterol. Those are the numbers most likely to be missed in your forties and fifties, and they're simple to check.
