The scariest part isn't forgetting. It's wondering what it means.

If you've lost a word, walked into a room blank, or read the same paragraph three times, you're not alone. Researchers have measured this in a big study of women going through the menopause transition.

What they found:

1. It showed up in testing
The SWAN study followed 2,362 women aged 42 to 52 for four years. During late perimenopause, their scores on processing speed and verbal memory tests didn't improve the way they did in other stages.

2. It came back
After menopause, performance returned to premenopausal levels. The authors said the difficulties may be time-limited.

3. Timing of hormone therapy showed a pattern
Women who started hormone therapy before their final period had better scores. This was observational, so it doesn't prove cause.

The honest limits: the effect was small. Many women report trouble thinking, but far fewer show impairment on tests. This study doesn't tell any one woman what's happening to her.

(Source: Greendale and colleagues, Neurology, 2009, from the SWAN study.)

See a doctor if it's getting worse over time, if it's affecting your work or safety, or if family members notice it. Bring a short list of examples with dates. That makes it much easier to tell what's going on.
