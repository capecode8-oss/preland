Nobody said juice was bad. The point is that it isn't free.

A lot of us gave up soda years ago and quietly swapped it for "healthy" orange juice. It feels like the right call. It has vitamin C. It's fruit. So how does it compare?

The numbers:

1. Cola
A 12 oz can has about 39 grams of sugar. That's about 3.2 grams per ounce.

2. Orange juice
An 8 oz glass has roughly 21 to 25 grams, depending on the brand. That's about 2.6 to 3.1 grams per ounce.

3. Per ounce, they're close
Per ounce, juice has roughly 80% to 95% of the sugar of cola. And a large glass is easy to drink without noticing.

The fair part: orange juice does come with vitamin C and potassium, and its sugar is naturally occurring rather than added. That matters. But without the fiber of the whole fruit, juice goes down fast, and the sugar arrives all at once.

You don't have to give it up. Notice that a big glass of juice can come close to a can of soda, and decide with the numbers in front of you.

(Figures are approximate: derived from USDA-based nutrition data and retail labels. Brands vary, so check yours.)

What I'd do: pour a smaller glass, or eat the orange. If you want something to drink, water with a squeeze of lemon works too. If you manage blood sugar or diabetes, ask your doctor how juice fits in.
