The concierge in Barcelona didn't tell me to watch my wallet. He told me to watch who's being nice to me.

I'd just asked him why a guest at the front desk had lost her phone in the plaza outside. Nobody grabbed it. Nobody ran. She handed it over herself.

"It's the new playbook," he said. "They don't take anymore. They offer. And it takes about four seconds."

I've been to 47 countries, and I had said yes to almost every one of these without thinking. Being polite felt safer than being rude. That's exactly what they count on.

Here are the four he told me to look for.

1. "Let me take your photo."
You hand over your phone, smiling. That's the whole trick. Take a selfie, or ask a family, not someone who walked up to you first.

2. The bracelet.
A stranger reaches for your wrist and ties a string on it. Then comes the ask for money. Once it's on, saying no gets awkward. Step back before it touches you.

3. "Is this yours?"
A gold ring on the ground, held out to you. Then a story, then a request for cash. It was never real. Keep walking.

4. "Oh no, you have something on your bag."
While one person wipes it off, another is already in your pocket. If someone points out a spill, take one step away and check your pockets yourself.

The rule he gave me fits in one sentence: a stranger who approaches you first has a reason, and it isn't you.

A polite no costs four seconds. A yes can cost you the trip.
