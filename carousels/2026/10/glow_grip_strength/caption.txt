Your blood pressure gets checked at every visit. Your grip strength almost never does.

That may be backwards. A big study called PURE measured grip strength in 139,691 adults aged 35 to 70 across 17 countries, then followed them over time.

What they found:

1. Weaker grip, higher risk
Every 5 kg drop in grip strength was linked to a 16% higher risk of death from any cause, and 17% higher for heart-related death.

2. It beat blood pressure
Grip strength was a stronger predictor of all-cause and heart-related death than systolic blood pressure.

3. It wasn't linked to everything
It didn't predict diabetes, pneumonia, COPD, or falls and fractures in this study.

(Source: The Lancet, 2015, the PURE study, as reported by Healio.)

Grip isn't something you can just train away. Researchers think it reflects your overall muscle and health. The practical takeaway: ask your doctor about it, and keep building strength with the things you already do, like carrying groceries, gardening and lifting.

One more detail: the study covered high-, middle- and low-income countries, and grip is cheap and quick to measure. It costs almost nothing, and it still almost never comes up at a normal appointment.
