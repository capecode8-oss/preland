The editors didn't talk about fear. They talked about habits.

The Points Guy asked its editors who travel alone for the small things they always do. None of it needs special gear or a big budget.

What they said:

1. Don't advertise that you're solo
One editor, Olivia Mittak, tells people she's meeting friends. She also writes down her hotel room number rather than saying it out loud when asked.

2. Be firm
Another, Lyndsey Matthews, says a strong, polite "no, thank you" works when someone seems to be scamming you or something feels unsafe.

3. Make a safety net
Annie Black tells any friends at her destination that she's visiting, even if she barely knows them. Several editors share their location through apps like Find My, and carry local cash for cash-only shops or broken ATMs.

(Source: The Points Guy, "solo travel safety tips." The article is the editors' own advice, not research.)

None of this is about being afraid of the world, and none of it makes you paranoid. You don't need to be fearful to be careful. The point of these habits is that they take almost no effort, and they make you a harder target by default.

On every trip I noticed the same thing. I planned carefully for strangers, taxis and hotels. I never planned for my own body: sleep, swelling, energy, what the flight did to me.
