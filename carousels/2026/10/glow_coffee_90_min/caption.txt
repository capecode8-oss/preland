If you wake at 3 AM, look at your last cup before you look at your first.

The "wait 90 minutes" rule is everywhere. The theory: cortisol naturally surges after you wake, so coffee too early interferes with it and sets up an afternoon crash.

What I found when I looked for the evidence:

1. Direct studies are thin
Coverage of the rule keeps repeating the same few ideas. Writers who looked for direct evidence that waiting prevents an afternoon crash found very little.

2. Daily drinkers adapt
In regular coffee drinkers, the cortisol response to caffeine fades over time, and can almost disappear at high daily intakes.

3. The mechanism that is solid
Caffeine blocks adenosine receptors. That part is well supported. Whether waiting 90 minutes helps is the part that isn't.

(Sources: Fortune and other secondary coverage. I couldn't find the primary studies, so treat this as popular advice, not settled science.)

What may matter more than the first cup: how much you drink and how late. Caffeine can stay in your system for hours, so a late cup can still be there at bedtime.

If you want to experiment, change one thing for a week: move your last cup earlier and see whether you fall asleep faster or wake less. One change, one week, then judge. That tells you more about your body than any rule from the internet.

I spent months tweaking when I drank coffee. I never asked what it did to the rest of my day.
