Who this plan is for

This plan is for women whose appetite is low, whether from a GLP-1 medicine, stress, illness recovery, a busy life, or simply getting older, and who want to keep protein up so their muscles are not what they lose.

This is education, not medical advice. Protein needs differ. If you have kidney disease, liver disease, diabetes, an eating disorder, or you are pregnant or breastfeeding, talk to your doctor or a registered dietitian before you change your protein intake. If you have lost weight without trying, or you cannot eat enough for days, tell your doctor.

Why protein, when you eat so little?

When you eat less, your body can use muscle for fuel. Protein at each meal gives your muscles a reason to stay. The standard U.S. recommendation is 0.8 grams per kilogram of body weight a day, which is the minimum to avoid deficiency. For people on GLP-1 medicines, expert groups commonly suggest more, often around 1.2 to 1.6 grams per kilogram a day, together with strength training. Your own number is a question for your doctor or dietitian. If you are on a GLP-1 medicine, the guide "GLP-1 Muscle Guard" has a table to calculate it.

The plan in one sentence: five or six very small meals a day, each one built around protein, mostly cold or very simple, so you can finish them even when you do not feel like eating.

The 5 rules

  1. Protein first. Eat the protein part before anything else.
  2. Small and often. Five or six mini-meals beat three big ones.
  3. Cold and bland is fine. Cold food has less smell and often feels better when you are nauseous.
  4. Eat by the clock. Set reminders, do not wait for hunger.
  5. Sip, do not gulp. Drink protein over 20–30 minutes, and drink water between meals, not in large amounts with them.

Sunday 30-minute prep

  • Hard-boil 6 to 8 eggs.
  • Portion yogurt or cottage cheese into small cups.
  • Cook or buy a rotisserie chicken, and pull it into 3 oz portions.
  • Portion almonds, cheese and berries into small bags.
  • Cook a pot of lentils or soup, and portion it.
